# ADR-R46: revoice the whole clip in one voice, fitted and lip synced, when patching would sound stitched

Date: 2026-11-07. Status: accepted (package v2-revoice-whole; the user's request and decisions below, approved mockup "revoice whole clip").

## Context

The user rewrites clips in their cloned voice. When many phrases change, patching each changed phrase into the real recording sounds stitched: each take differs from the real voice and from the other takes at every join. ADR-R45's per-video voice narrows the gap but can't close it at the joins. The user asked for an option that generates the whole clip's voice with AI and lip-syncs the video, so there are no seams.

The user decided (2026-11-07):

1. **Lip-sync model: both, Claude picks.** The default is `fal-ai/sync-lipsync/v2:lipsync-2` (about $3 a minute). Review offers sync-3 (`fal-ai/sync-lipsync/v3`, about $8 a minute) as a priced upgrade that re-runs only the lip sync over the paid voice. The Choose card prices the default.
2. **Claude mode switches automatically within the spend cap.** In ✦ Claude does it all, VoxClip revoices the whole clip instead of patching when the rule below says the patch would sound stitched and the whole clip's price fits what is left of the run's cap. Over the cap Claude patches as before and offers "Revoice the Whole Clip · ≈ $X" inline; nothing is spent until it is clicked. Outside Claude mode the Rewrite panel asks once per clip, at the price step, and keeps the pick per clip.

## Decision

1. **The switch rule** (`WholeClipRevoice.reason`, shared constants, integer arithmetic so each edge is exact). Revoice the whole clip when either:
   - (a) the included voice changes re-voice at least **40%** of the clip's spoken words (`wordSharePercent`). The re-voiced count is the voice budget's: every new word of the included voice changes (`VoiceLabPatchBudget`). The spoken words are the clip's `word` items after its cuts. `revoiced × 100 ≥ spoken × 40`.
   - (b) at least **2** phrases can't fit their slot (`noFitPhrases`): after takes, the phrases `ClipTakePlan.fitsNoDuration` would keep as recorded; before takes, the phrases the free wording fit kept as recorded because their wording is too long for the video (ADR-R42).
   A whole clip escapes the slot limit because the picture is fitted to the voice.
2. **Claude mode's choice** (`WholeClipRevoice.autoChoice`): no reason → patch; no price in the tables → patch, never offered; price within the run's remaining cap → revoice; otherwise patch and offer. The over-cap offer's click approves exactly the offered price.
3. **The pipeline reuses Rewrite's own paths.**
   - **Script** (free): the clip's words in transcript order with every cut, correction and included rewrite applied (`WholeClipScript.assemble`).
   - **Voice:** the script split at sentence boundaries into parts of at most 12 s of source and 1,000 characters (`WholeClipScript.parts`), handed to a `ClipTakePlan` as voice changes (`WholeClipScript.hunks`). Each part goes through window placement, the R40 guard (before any price), R43 calibration, the R45 per-video voice with the anchor fallback, the take checks and the take journals. Placement joins touching windows, so a clip's parts are voiced as one window: one take per contiguous stretch.
   - **Fit** (free): the voice's length against the clip as edited (update 2026-11-08 below; originally the windows' source length). Clip speed scales voice and picture alike, so the ratio is the rendered one. Within **±8%** (`fitTolerance`) the voice is laid out on the clip as edited; a shorter voice is laid out too. A voice more than 8% longer asks Rewrite's measured-wording round (`voice-rewrite-shorter-measured.json`, the whole clip as one line with its measured and video seconds) for a shorter wording of about `words ÷ factor` words, at most **2** rounds (`maximumRewordRounds`), each checked before it is voiced again. After that the panel says so inline and offers Voice Only the Changed Phrases and Try Again.
   - **Lip sync:** one job over the merged window through `RewriteLipsyncSession`, `LipsyncRun`, the import, validation, conform, receipts and journals, with the chosen model (`LipsyncRun.execute(model:)`, `FalLipsyncProvider.prepareApprovedUpload(model:)`). The recipe records the model the job ran (`addingLipsync(model:)`).
4. **Never paid twice** (`WholeClipRevoiceLedger`). A voice is keyed by its script, voice record and model, and a lip sync by its voice, retime and model. Try Again after a cancelled or failed lip sync pays only the lip sync. The sync-3 upgrade pays only sync-3. A new wording is a new voice.
5. **Cancel and failure keep the clip.** Nothing enters the project before Use Whole Clip. Use is one undo step: Rewrite's own commit of the parts' segments, with their matched and lip-synced recipes, plus the project's record.
6. **The project's record** (`voiceLab.wholeClipRevoices`, schema v3 and v4, omitted when empty): `id`, `clipId`, `segmentIds`, `script`, `consentRecordId`, `videoRetime` (within ±8%), `lipsyncModel` (one of the two), `usd`, `match` (`take`, `anchorBaseline`, `realSpeech`) and `createdAt`. Every key is written; `usd` and `match` may be null. Validation requires the clip and every segment (each a text-to-speech segment in at most one record). An edit that removes the clip or one of its segments, or replaces the transcript, drops the record in the same step (`WholeClipRevoice.pruneDangling`, C# `PruneDangling`: removing a clip, the reference clean on save and Windows load, deleting a segment); the segments stay as voiced patches. A file that arrives with a dangling record is still refused.
7. **Disclosure.** A segment named by a record adds the disclosure kind `revoice`. The burned-in chip reads "Voice and lip movement generated with AI" and the post text "Voice and lip movement generated with AI." The rest of the label plan is unchanged, so a revoiced clip, being over 30% AI, is labelled throughout. Consent is the existing voice consent, plus the lip-sync approval.
8. **The stage** shows its AI voice tag over the whole revoiced clip, the pauses between parts included (`WholeClipRevoice.isRevoiced`).

### Update 2026-11-08: parts tile the clip

On the user's 0:29.7 clip (61 items, 14 cut fillers and tightened pauses), two of four parts were refused with `seamFadeCrossesCut`: each began right after a cut word, so its left seam's fade had no kept audio before it. The run stopped with the lip-sync copy ("The job could not be completed…"), wrote no log line, and Try Again · ≈ $1.90 failed the same way at once.

- **Parts tile the clip** (voice-lab §8.14 item 3). The first part starts at the clip's first item and the last ends at its last. At each boundary, the items between the last kept source word of one part and the first kept source word of the next go to the side of the widest gap, so both seams sit on kept audio. A cut pause counts as no gap. When no gap of `SEAM_MIN_GAP` exists there, the two parts become one change. Tiling alone was not enough: a tightened pause keeps no breath once both of its neighbours are replaced, and a cut run that touches both sentences leaves no gap at all. Joining is the smallest correct change, because placement already joins touching windows. The patch geometry (§8.4) is unchanged.
- **A failure says why and is logged.** It names the part and its reason; only lip-sync errors use the lip-sync copy. One `voice-lab` line per failure gives the step, the error case and the part.
- **Try Again is offered only when it can succeed.** After a stop that would only repeat (placement, or anything before a paid request), the card offers Voice Only the Changed Phrases with no price. Claude mode patches on its own and says so in the review.

### Update 2026-11-08: one timeline, never pad

On the user's 0:31.4 clip (MVP44, 49 words, eleven cut words and ten tightened pauses), Revoice the Whole Clip measured a 27 s voice against the parts' windows in source time, 48.6 s, pauses the edit tightens included. The Fit step saw a factor of 0.56 and asked for a longer wording of about `words ÷ factor` words, padding the script with words nobody said. The AI kept returning wordings of 50–69 words, each was voiced again, and after three paid rounds the step stopped with `cannotFit`. It spent about $0.22 and never reached the lip sync.

- **One timeline: the clip as edited.** The fit, the take's placement, the picture, the render, the lip sync and its price all use the clip after its cuts and tightened pauses. That is the length the user sees on the card and in the timeline, and the video the lip sync gets. The source time under the parts includes everything the user removed, so measuring against it would make every edited clip look too long for its voice.
  - **Fit.** `videoSeconds` is each part's window without the clip's own cuts inside it (`WholeClipRevoice.editedCuts` over the clip's output plan with no voice, `editedSeconds`). The voice is the takes' speech between their trims, before any pause the layout adds.
  - **Placement and render.** Each take is fitted to its window as edited, not to the window's source (`VoiceLabEditedLayout` on the take input, `VoiceLabNaturalPacing.layout`, the fit planner's `targetSamples`). Its picture is a `cut` recipe holding the clip's own cuts, at zoom 1 with no punch-in and no edge guard (`EDITED_CUT_ZOOM`, `validEditedCuts`, up to `MAXIMUM_EDITED_CUTS` = 512, the schema's `cuts.maxItems`). A pause's jump cut keeps its one or two cuts, its 0.3 s guards and its 1.06× punch-in. The render then plays the clip's edited length with the voice over it, and the lip sync's window maps through those cuts to the same output.
  - **Lip sync.** It is quoted on the clip as edited plus Rewrite's one-second handles, and the job is priced again from its real window, as before.
  - Before this update the take filled the window's source and the picture was retimed (`zoomCover`, 0.80–1.25×), so a 27 s voice over 48.6 s could never be matched. The picture is no longer retimed this way: the record's `videoRetime` was 1 for a laid-out voice until the MVP45 update below made it the picture's even speed-up. Earlier records keep the retime they ran.
- **Keep the recording's rhythm.** The take keeps its own speech and pauses, in order, from the clip's first word. When it is shorter than the clip as edited, the difference first becomes pause at sentence ends, then silence at its end; whatever is left trims the picture's end.
  - **The pause bound** (`layoutPauseProfile`, `layoutPauseBound`). A sentence-end pause grows to at most the project's pause threshold (`edits.pauseThresholdSeconds`, 0.8 s on the user's clip). A longer pause is one the clip's own Tighten would cut. It may shrink to the punctuated floor, 150 ms. Pauses inside a sentence keep their length. After the last word the voice may end on the clip's own last pause, up to the same bound.
  - **Beyond the bound the picture is trimmed.** The frames after that last pause go, latest first (`layoutCuts`), so the clip ends a little earlier. A voice that runs a little long gives back cut picture, from the latest cut, until it fits.
  - Within ±8% the step reads "video matched (−1.3%)". A shorter voice reads "laid out on the clip's pauses (−14.0%)". The even retime is not used: the clip as edited is already a jump-cut picture, and a `cut` recipe carries no retime.
  - **Sentence positions are not anchored.** The take is one stretch of speech, as placement joins the parts into one window. Its sentences keep the take's own spacing plus the bounded growth, not each original sentence's position in the clip. The lip sync redraws the mouth over the whole clip.
- **Never pad.** `WholeClipRevoice.fit` asks for a wording only when the voice is more than 8% longer than the clip as edited. It then asks for a **shorter** one of about `words ÷ factor` words (`.reword(targetWords:)`, at most 2 rounds). A shorter voice is laid out at any round (`.laidOut`), and the prompt never asks for more words.
- **Check before paying** (`rewordFits`, `predictedSeconds`). The reworded script is said only when its speaking-rate estimate fits. The estimate is the measured voice scaled by the ratio of the two scripts' estimates (`VoiceLabPhraseTiming.estimate` at the project's rate), so the rate's own error cancels. The prediction must come in at or under the clip as edited plus 8%. Otherwise the step stops inline with no new voice: "The shorter wording would still run about 0:35.3 for 0:31 of video, so no new voice was made."
- **Try Again doesn't repeat a failure.** After a Fit stop (the voice can't fit, or a reword came back too long), with the clip's script unchanged, the stopped card's primary is **Voice Only the Changed Phrases** and Try Again comes second. Once the script changes, Try Again leads again. The voice already paid is reused either way.
- **Claude mode** runs the same session, so the same fit applies. A Fit stop patches the changed phrases on its own, as a placement stop does. What its voice cost is charged to the run, and the patch gets what is left.
- **Windows** ports the pure rules to C# Core: the fit, check before paying, `LayoutCuts`, `EditedCuts`, `EditedSeconds`, `LayoutPauseBound`, the edited form of `ValidCuts`, and `VoiceLabVideoFraming`'s zoom-1 spans. Both Cores replay `whole-clip-fit-cases.json` (`voxclip-whole-clip-fit/1`), including the user's clip as numbers, and `whole-clip-revoice-cases.json`'s updated `fit`. The layout fit itself (`VoiceLabNaturalPacing.layout`) is Mac only, like the run.

### Update 2026-11-08: your own pace, and Fit checks the picture (MVP45)

The user's MVP45 run read "Clip 1 · 0:31.4 · 50 words": Script ✓ 52 words, Voice ✓ 0:20.2 in one take, Fit ✓ "0:20.2 for 0:31.3 of video, laid out on the clip's pauses (−35.4%)", then "Couldn't lip sync: The job could not be completed." on every Try Again. The log had only `whole clip failure step=lipsync type=WholeClipRevoiceSession.Stop case=noVoice`, with no submission: it failed on the Mac, before fal.ai.

- **The real cause.** The source is a 3840 × 2160 DJI file. `LipsyncWindowPreparation.prepare` refused any oriented side above the compositor's 1920-pixel bound with `LipsyncPreparationError.unsupportedVideo`. `RewriteLipsyncSession.reason` had no copy for it, so it showed the generic line, and `wholeClipLipsync` rethrew it as `Stop.noVoice`. The refusal is deterministic, so every Try Again repeated it. A second, latent stale state: after a failed video preparation the plan kept `videoPreparationFailure`, so the next picture match skipped preparing and stopped at once on the old problem.
- **The fix.** The window is sent at the source's oriented size scaled to the bound, aspect kept (`LipsyncWindowPreparation.uploadSize`, as a source-shaped export: 1920 × 1080 here). It is the clean picture: no clip controls, effects, captions or music, which the compositor applies once over the lip-synced frames. Each picture match forgets an earlier failed preparation (`retryVideoPreparation`).
- **Fit checks the picture.** Fit passes only when the picture can be matched to the laid-out voice and sent: every part's cut recipe, speed-up and trims prepared through Rewrite's duration path, then the lip sync's free checks on this Mac (the upload size, a fal.ai key, a merged window fal.ai takes, its price). Otherwise it stops at Fit with that reason, makes no lip-sync call, and the patch is the way on. A missing fal.ai key is checked before the voice is paid for.
- **A stop says what failed.** A picture that can't be matched is `Stop.pictureUnmatched(part:)` or `Unmatched(error:)` wrapping the preparation's own error; a lip sync that fails is `LipsyncFailed(error:local:)` with the job's own error. The log line names the real type and case, such as `type=LipsyncPreparationError case=unsupportedVideo`. A stop that would only repeat (a local lip-sync failure, a picture that can't be matched) offers no priced Try Again. The same stop twice in a row makes the patch the primary, with Try Again second.
- **Your own pace (the user's decision, 2026-11-08: "keep the voice as generated and speed the video 1..1.2 is mostly fine").** The previous update dropped "natural pace" for whole-clip takes, so the user's slow, paused speech came back as a 20.2 s voice for 31.3 s of clip.
  - The voice is kept exactly as generated: `eleven_v4` at provider pace 1.0, never stretched, priced per character as before.
  - When it is shorter than the clip as edited, the picture is first sped up evenly by the clip ÷ the voice's natural length, from 1× to at most `maximumPictureSpeed` = 1.2× (`WholeClipRevoice.pictureSpeed`). The take is laid out on the sped-up picture's length, so the sentence-end pauses (within the bound) and then the trim take only what the speed-up can't. A voice as long or longer keeps today's rules: a shorter wording only, and the shrinking pauses.
  - The picture carries both: a `cut` recipe at zoom 1 with the clip's own cuts and `retimeFactor` = kept source ÷ output (`validEditedCuts`: above 1, at most 1.2, within 1e-6). The output plan, the lip-sync window and both platforms' video spans map the kept source evenly (`VoiceLabOutputPlan.cutScale`). A pause's jump cut never speeds up.
  - The Fit line names it: "Fit · 0:20.2 for 0:31.3 of video, picture 1.20× and laid out on the clip's pauses", or "picture 1.12× to fit" when the speed-up alone fills the clip. A speed-up under 1.005× keeps the plain verdict.
  - The lip sync gets the sped-up picture and is priced on its output length plus the handles. The record's `videoRetime` is the picture's speed, valid from 0.92 to 1.2 (the schema and both Cores; earlier records keep their retime).
  - **The user's clip** (the MVP44/45 clip's numbers, 31.2 s as edited from its first word to its last, a 20.2 s take of its 10 sentences; `WholeClipPictureSpeedTests`). Before: laid out at 1×, 24.1 s of output (the voice, 3.1 s of sentence-pause growth and 0.8 s at the end), 7.0 s of the clip trimmed past its own cuts, and the lip sync priced on the clip as edited plus the handles, 33.2 s (≈ $1.66). After: picture 1.20×, the same 24.1 s of voice and pauses over 28.9 s of picture, 2.2 s trimmed, and the lip sync priced on 26.1 s (≈ $1.31). The sped cut validates and the picture match passes.
  - **Options owed, not built.** S1: `eleven_v4` with `voice_settings.speed` through the text-to-speech endpoint (Text to Dialogue has no speed field; voice-lab §8.5 refuses a pace other than 1.0 on it), owed to a live probe. "Multilingual v2 when paced": a model switch for a slower provider pace, owed to a user decision. The local pitch-preserving time stretch was considered and replaced by this rule before it was built.
- **Rewrite's phrase takes are unchanged.** `VoiceLabEditedLayout.maximumPictureSpeed` defaults to 1; only the whole clip passes 1.2. A pause's jump cut still refuses any `retimeFactor`, and phrase pace stays 1.0 on v4.
- **Claude mode** runs the same session and patches on its own after any stop that would only repeat, the voice's cost charged to the run.
- **Windows** ports the rule, the copy, the record's range, the sped edited cut and its even mapping and framing to C# Core; both Cores replay `whole-clip-speed-cases.json` (`voxclip-whole-clip-speed/1`).

## Consequences

- A heavily rewritten clip can sound like one take, at a lip-sync price: about $1.55 for 31 s with lipsync-2, about $4.13 with sync-3.
- **The whole clip's lip-sync seconds are quoted** from the script's span on the clip as edited plus Rewrite's one-second handles (update 2026-11-08). The job is priced again from its real window, which can differ by the seam widening.
- **Parts as written.** A clip whose script is one stretch of speech is voiced as one take: placement joins the parts. The mockup's "3 parts, one take" applies only when parts stay apart. The step reads "one take" otherwise.
- **A whole-recording clip** has no speech beside it to level the voice against (voice-lab §8.7 item 2), and the Voice step stops with that reason. The same holds for any patch over a whole recording.
- **Windows owes** the twin: the record's wire, validation and schema replay, the `revoice` disclosure kind, the rule and autoChoice, the card and run, and Claude mode's switch. Until then Windows refuses to open a project holding a record, as it refuses any `voiceLab` key it doesn't know (`ProjectValidation`, apps/windows). A project with no whole-clip revoice is unchanged on disk, since the key is omitted.
