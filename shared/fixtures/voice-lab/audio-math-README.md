# Voice Lab audio math vectors

`audio-math-cases.json` holds the shared vectors for the pure audio measurements in both Cores: `VoiceLabLoudness` (voiced level and gain, sample and true peak, momentary loudness, seam jump and flatness) and `VoiceLabConform` (the duration-conform decision). They follow voice-lab.md §4.4, §7.3, §8.6, §8.7 item 2 and §8.11 items 2 and 3, as pinned below. The Mac suite `apps/macos/Tests/VoxClipCoreTests/VoiceLab/VoiceLabAudioMathTests.swift` and the Windows suite `apps/windows/tests/VoxClip.Core.Tests/Tracks/voice-lab/VoiceLabAudioMathTests.cs` read every case and fail on an unknown kind.

Regenerate only with `python3 tools/fixtures/voice-lab/generate_audio_math.py`, using a Python that has NumPy, and review the diff as a behaviour change. The generator is an independent NumPy reference of the same rules; the tests never run it. Two runs give byte-identical output. Expected values are never edited by hand.

## Signals

Signals are stored as parameters, never as samples. A signal is `{"rate", "parts", "gainDb"}`, and its parts are concatenated in order. Every part has a `samples` count:

- `sine`: `amplitude × sin(2π × frequency × n / rate + phase)`, with `amplitude = 10^(peakDbfs / 20)` and `n` counted from the part's first sample.
- `silence`: zeros.
- `noise`: the 32-bit LCG `x(n+1) = (1664525 × x(n) + 1013904223) mod 2^32`, starting from `x(0) = seed`. Sample `n` uses `x(n+1)` as `x ÷ 2^31 − 1`, multiplied by `10^(peakDbfs / 20)`.

The optional `gainDb` (default 0) multiplies the whole signal by `10^(gainDb / 20)`; `voiced-gain-step-48k` and the short-chunk conform outputs use it. Each sample is computed in Float64 in exactly this order, `(amplitude × waveform) × gain`, with the sine argument evaluated left to right as written, and is then rounded once to Float32 (round to nearest, ties to even).

## Case kinds

| `kind` | Input | `expected` |
|---|---|---|
| `kWeighting` | `rate` | `shelf` and `highPass`, each `[b0, b1, b2, a1, a2]` with a0 = 1 |
| `voiced` | `signal` | `voicedRmsDb`; null when no frame is voiced |
| `peaks` | `signal` | `oversampling`, `samplePeakDbfs` and `truePeakDbtp`; null is −∞ |
| `neighbourhood` | `signal`, `window` | `voicedRmsDb` of the source beside the window, or `error` |
| `levelGain` | `source`, `window`, `candidate` | `sourceVoicedRmsDb` (the neighbourhood level), `candidateVoicedRmsDb`, `candidateTruePeakDbtp`, and `gainDb` or `error` |
| `momentary` | `signal`, `windowStart` | `lufs` of the 400 ms window at `windowStart`; null is −∞ |
| `seamJump` | `signal`, `seams` | `jumps` per seam and `lufsJumpLu`, or `error` |
| `flatness` | `signal` | `windows` kept, population `stdev` and `flatness` |
| `conform` | `input`, `output`, `searchSeconds` | `kind` with `delta`, or with `lag` and `peak` |
| `conformEnvelope` | `rate`, `inputLevels`, `outputLevels`, `inputSamples`, `outputSamples`, `searchSeconds` | as `conform` |

For `conform` cases each side's envelope is the 10 ms RMS of its rendered signal: frames of `round_half_up(rate × 0.01)` samples from sample 0, the partial final frame omitted, each RMS computed in Float64 and stored as Float32, as `AudioEnvelope` holds it. `conformEnvelope` gives the levels directly, to pin exact ties. A `window` is `[start, end)` in source samples. `error` is `noVoicedAudio` or `insufficientContext`.

`tolerances` gives the comparison bounds: ±0.01 for dB, LUFS and LU values, ±0.05 dB for true peak, ±0.001 for flatness, ±1e-9 for correlation peaks and ±1e-12 for filter coefficients. Window counts, lags, deltas, decision kinds and errors must match exactly. `publishedKWeighting48k` holds the BS.1770 coefficients that the 48 kHz derivation meets within 1e-6.

## Rules the vectors pin

- **Frames.** `round_half_up(rate × 0.01)` samples from sample 0; a trailing partial frame is ignored. A frame is voiced when its RMS exceeds 10^(−45/20). `voicedRmsDb` is 20·log10 of the square root of the mean square over every sample of the voiced frames.
- **Neighbourhood.** The source level for a window `[start, end)` takes `[start − N, start)` and `[end, end + N)` of the source, with N = `round_half_up(3 × rate)` (`LOUDNESS_NEIGHBOURHOOD`), each clamped to the take. The window itself is excluded, because it is what gets replaced. Each side is framed on its own grid from its first sample, and its trailing partial frame is ignored. The voiced frames of both sides, before side first, pool into one mean square. When neither side has a voiced frame the result is `noVoicedAudio`. This is the lead's decision on §8.7 item 2.
- **Gain.** `min(clamp(sourceVoicedDb − candidateVoicedDb, −12, +12), −1 − candidateTruePeakDbtp)`, where `sourceVoicedDb` is the neighbourhood level beside the case's `window`. Either side without a voiced frame is `noVoicedAudio`.
- **Peaks.** Sample peak is 20·log10(max |x|). True peak uses the ITU-R BS.1770-4 Annex 2 48-tap, 4-phase interpolation filter: all four phases below 96 kHz, phases 0 and 2 from 96 kHz up to 192 kHz, and the sample peak from 192 kHz. Annex 2 publishes only the 4× filter, so 2× uses its even phases. The buffer is silent outside its bounds and every output of the full convolution counts, so an abrupt start or end rings like any other step. No phase lands on an input sample, and phases 1 and 2 pass DC at 0.973, so the filter alone can read below the samples; the true peak is the larger of the interpolated peak and the sample peak, the lead's decision after the round-1 review. `peaks-click-*` reads 0 dBTP where the filter alone gives −0.245 dB. Both rules are conservative: a higher true peak can only lower the gain cap. In `inter-sample-peak-*` below 192 kHz the largest output lies in the start transient (+0.083 dBTP); the interior inter-sample peak is +0.045 dBTP. Both are above the −3.01 dBFS sample peak. `inter-sample-peak-192000` reads its −3.01 dBFS sample peak, because 192 kHz is not oversampled. `peaks-crest-end-*` stops a −6 dBFS tone on its crest and reads about 1 dB above its sample peak, where `peaks-zero-end-96k` stops at a zero crossing; `gain-edge-ringing-cap-48k` shows the cap that results.
- **K-weighting.** Both biquads come from the bilinear transform prewarped at f0: the shelf at f0 1681.974450955533 Hz, gain 3.999843853973347 dB and Q 0.7071752369554196 with mid-band numerator Vh^0.4996667741545416, and the RLB high-pass at f0 38.13547087602444 Hz and Q 0.5003270373238773 with the unnormalised numerator [1, −2, 1]. Each runs in direct form I from zero state at the buffer's first sample, shelf first. This is libebur128's derivation (MIT); the published 48 kHz coefficients are the normative anchor, and the `kWeighting` cases pin it from 16 to 192 kHz. The 997 Hz momentary cases stay within 0.05 LU of −3.01 LUFS across that range.
- **Momentary loudness.** −0.691 + 10·log10 of the K-weighted mean square over `round_half_up(0.4 × rate)` samples, with channel weight 1. `momentary-click-*` pins the window to the sample with a full-scale click just before it, on its first sample and on its last. The K-weighting runs from sample 0, so the samples before the window set the filter state.
- **Seam jump.** The windows are `[seam − W, seam)` and `[seam, seam + W)` on the buffer's grid, shortened at its edges. A shortened window below `round_half_up(0.1 × rate)` samples is `insufficientContext`. The jump is |max(M_after, −70) − max(M_before, −70)|: each window's loudness is floored at −70 LUFS, the BS.1770 absolute gate, so the jump is always finite (the lead's decision on §8.11 item 3). Two windows at the floor give 0. A digitally silent side counts as −70 LUFS, so a cut from room tone to silence reads the room tone's distance from the floor (`seam-room-tone-to-silence-48k`, 8.3 LU) and QA can catch it. `lufsJumpLu` is the largest jump, and 0 without seams. `seam-click-before-48k` puts a click on the last sample before the seam: the after window reads only its ringing, carried across the seam by the filter state.
- **Flatness.** Windows of W samples start every `round_half_up(0.1 × rate)` samples from sample 0, whole windows only. Windows below −70 LUFS are dropped. Flatness is 1 − min(1, population stdev ÷ 6), and 1 with fewer than two windows.
- **Conform.** Δ = outputSamples − inputSamples, and T = `round_half_up(0.020 × rate)`. When |Δ| ≤ T the result is `trimOrPad(Δ)`. Otherwise lags run over whole frames up to `floor(round_half_up(searchSeconds × rate) ÷ frame)`. Each lag's score is the Pearson correlation of the overlapping envelope frames, with both means taken over that overlap, clamped to at most 1; this is the lead's reading of §4.4's "normalised cross-correlation". In `conform-pearson-loud-lead-in-48k`, three loud frames before the output's speech leave the overlap at the true lag identical to the input, where means over the whole envelopes would read 0.47 and reject it. A constant overlap scores 0, and so does an overlap shorter than `ceil(min(nIn, nOut) / 2)` frames, where nIn and nOut are the envelope lengths. That minimum is the lead's decision after the round-1 review: two frames correlate ±1 by construction, so without it a short chunk's edge lag beat the true alignment (`conform-short-chunk-48k`, `conform-reach-plus-two-frames-48k`). The first strictly larger score wins in the order 0, −1, +1, −2, +2, …, so ties go to the smallest |lag| and then to the negative lag. A peak below 0.5, or |Δ − L| > T, is `outOfSync`; otherwise the result is `shift`. A positive lag means the output is late. The boundaries are pinned: |Δ − L| of exactly T is accepted and T + 1 is not (`conform-offset-*-tolerance-48k`), a peak of exactly 0.5 passes and 0.49975 does not (`conform-floor-*`), and the reach is floored, so at 22.05 kHz, with 221-sample frames, ±0.3 s searches 29 frames, not 30 (`conform-reach-floor-22k`).
