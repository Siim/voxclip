This morning I opened the window and listened to the sounds outside. A bicycle rolled along the path, a dog waited by the gate, and someone was carrying a basket of apples. The sky was bright, but the air still felt cool. I put a kettle on the stove and found a clean cup on the shelf.

There is a small garden behind the house. In spring we plant beans and carrots there. In summer the leaves make a quiet rustling sound. When the rain stops, I like to walk along the fence and see what has changed. Sometimes a new flower has opened. Sometimes the soil only needs a little water.

Later I will take a notebook to the park. I want to describe the colours of the trees and the shapes of the clouds, without rushing. A good story can begin with something ordinary: a lost glove, a friendly question, or a train arriving at a station. I will leave room on the page for another idea.

For now I am speaking in my usual voice, clearly and comfortably. I can pause to breathe between sentences. The words do not need to sound dramatic or perfect. If there is still time, I can describe my garden or read the first paragraph again. What matters most is speaking naturally, as I would when telling a friend about my day. Small things can be easy to notice when I give them my quiet attention. At the end of this reading I will sit quietly for a moment, then stop the recording.
